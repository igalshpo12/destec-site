🧠 Chapter 1

What Is AI, Really?

Strip away the sci-fi hype and understand what artificial intelligence actually is — and what it isn't.

Artificial Intelligence is software that can perform tasks we once thought required human intelligence — understanding language, recognizing images, making decisions, generating content. But it works very differently from how a human brain works.

Think of it this way: a human learns to speak by growing up in a world full of conversations. An AI learns by reading an enormous amount of text — billions of web pages, books, articles — and finding patterns in how words, sentences, and ideas connect to each other.

💡

The Core Idea

AI doesn't "understand" the way you do. It's an extremely sophisticated pattern-matching machine. When it gives you a great answer, it's because it has seen millions of similar questions and learned what good answers look like.

🤖

Narrow AI

AI built for one specific task. A spam filter, a chess engine, face recognition — each is brilliant at one thing and useless at everything else. This is what almost all real-world AI is today.

🌐

General AI (AGI)

A hypothetical AI that can reason across any topic the way a human can. We don't have this yet — despite what headlines say. Modern AI assistants are very advanced narrow AI, not AGI.

📝

Generative AI

AI that creates new content — text, images, code, audio, video. Claude, ChatGPT, Midjourney all fall here. This is the category that exploded in 2022–2025.

A Quick History (In Plain English)

1

1950s–1980s · The Rules Era

Programmers tried to write every single rule an AI needed. "If the user says X, respond with Y." This hit a wall fast — the real world has too many exceptions.

2

1990s–2010s · Machine Learning Takes Over

Instead of writing rules, engineers gave AI huge amounts of data and let it figure out the rules itself. This worked for specific tasks like email spam detection and image recognition.

3

2017 · The Transformer Breakthrough

Google published a paper titled "Attention Is All You Need." It introduced a new architecture called the Transformer — the engine that powers every modern AI assistant, including Claude and ChatGPT.

4

2022–Now · The GenAI Explosion

ChatGPT launched in November 2022 and reached 100 million users in 2 months — the fastest product adoption in history. AI became mainstream practically overnight. We're still in this era.

⚙️ Chapter 2

How Language Models Actually Work

You don't need a computer science degree. These are the concepts that actually matter — explained with analogies, not equations.

The Autocomplete That Went to University

At its core, an LLM predicts the next most likely word (or piece of a word). But it was trained on so much human knowledge that "the next likely word" ends up being deeply insightful, creative, and contextually aware. It's autocomplete that read basically everything ever written.

∞
🧩

Tokens

AI doesn't read word by word. It breaks text into tokens — chunks that are roughly ¾ of a word on average. "Unbelievable" might be 3 tokens. "AI" is 1. This matters because models have a limit on how many tokens they can handle at once (the "context window").

🔢

Parameters

These are the "settings" adjusted during training — the numbers the model learned. GPT-4 has an estimated 1.8 trillion. Claude's exact count is private. More parameters generally (but not always) means more capable. Think of them as the model's memory of patterns.

📏

Context Window

The maximum amount of text the model can "see" at once — your entire conversation, plus any documents you share. Claude 3.5 Sonnet has a 200,000 token context window (~150,000 words). Larger = better at long tasks.

🌡️

Temperature

A dial controlling how random the output is. Temperature 0 = always picks the most likely next token (predictable, factual). Temperature 1 = more creative and varied. Most apps set this for you behind the scenes.

🎯

Training vs. Fine-tuning

Training is the massive initial process of learning from billions of documents. Fine-tuning is additional training on a smaller, specific dataset to specialize the model (e.g., making it better at legal documents or medical terminology).

🔄

RLHF

Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback. After initial training, humans rate the model's responses. These ratings teach the model to prefer helpful, harmless, and honest answers. This is why Claude doesn't just generate random text — it learned what "good" looks like.

What an LLM Can and Can't Do

Capability LLM Can Do This? Notes
Write, summarize, translate text✓ ExcellentCore strength — this is what they're built for
Answer factual questions~ OftenCan be wrong ("hallucinate") — always verify important facts
Write and debug code✓ Very GoodStrong in Python, JS, TypeScript; weaker in obscure languages
Reason step-by-step✓ GoodBetter when you explicitly ask it to "think step by step"
Browse the internet in real-time✗ No**Some versions have web search as an add-on tool
Remember past conversations✗ NoEach conversation starts fresh unless using memory features/Projects
Perform complex math reliably~ PartialGood at simple math; for complex, use code execution or a calculator
Understand images (with vision models)✓ YesClaude, GPT-4o, Gemini can analyze images you share
Know events after its training cutoff✗ NoModels have a knowledge cutoff date; they don't know recent news
⚠️

Hallucinations — The Most Important Thing to Know

LLMs can confidently state things that are completely made up. They generate plausible-sounding text, not necessarily true text. For anything important — medical, legal, financial, factual — always verify with a reliable external source. Think of the AI as a very smart friend who sometimes misremembers things but won't admit it.

✦ Chapter 3

Meet Claude

Who made Claude, what makes it different, and how to get the most out of it — from a total beginner's perspective.

🏢

Who is Anthropic?

Anthropic is an AI safety company founded in 2021 by former OpenAI researchers, including Dario and Daniela Amodei. Their mission is to build AI systems that are safe, beneficial, and understandable. Claude is their primary AI assistant product.


Anthropic differs from competitors in its emphasis on AI safety research — they publish papers on making AI less deceptive, more interpretable, and aligned with human values.

🛡️

Constitutional AI

Anthropic invented a training technique called Constitutional AI (CAI). Instead of relying solely on human raters, they gave Claude a set of principles (a "constitution") and trained it to evaluate its own responses against those principles.


The result: Claude is trained to be Helpful, Harmless, and Honest — what Anthropic calls the "3 H's." This shapes how Claude responds even when you push it in uncomfortable directions.

The Claude Model Family

Anthropic releases multiple versions of Claude. Here's what each tier is for:

👑
Claude Opus
Most Capable

The most powerful Claude model. Best for complex analysis, nuanced writing, intricate coding, and tasks that require deep reasoning. Slower and more expensive to run.

Premium
⚖️
Claude Sonnet
Best Balance — Recommended

The sweet spot. Extremely capable, fast, and cost-effective. This is what most people use for everyday tasks, coding, writing, and analysis. Claude.ai free tier uses a limited version.

Free + Pro
⚡

Fastest & Lightest

The smallest, fastest, cheapest Claude model. Great for simpler tasks, real-time applications, or when you need speed over depth. Often used by developers building apps.

API / Developer

Claude's Killer Features

📁

Projects

Give Claude persistent context — upload documents, set a system prompt, and it remembers the context across all conversations in that project. Perfect for work with recurring needs (e.g., always knowing your company's style guide).

🎨

Artifacts

Claude can create self-contained deliverables — code, HTML pages, markdown documents, SVG graphics — that appear alongside the chat. You can see, run, and download them instantly. This guide is an Artifact.

👁️

Vision (Image Understanding)

Share any image and Claude will analyze it — diagrams, screenshots, photos, charts. Great for explaining what's in a photo, reading a whiteboard, or analyzing a graph from a report.

💻

Claude Code

A specialized version of Claude that runs in your terminal or IDE. It can read your actual codebase, write and edit files, run commands, and fix bugs — like a developer pair-programmer who works at the speed of AI.

🌟

What Claude Is Especially Good At (vs. Other AI)

  • Long documents — its 200K token context window means it can read entire books or codebases
  • Nuanced writing — tone, style, and subtlety tend to feel more human
  • Careful reasoning — it's trained to say "I don't know" rather than hallucinate confidently
  • Following complex instructions — handles multi-part, detailed prompts better than most
  • Code quality — writes clean, well-structured code with explanations
💬 Chapter 4

Mastering Prompts

The difference between a mediocre and a brilliant AI response is almost always the prompt. Here's how to unlock the full potential.

🎯

The Golden Rule of Prompting

Treat AI like a brilliant new employee who just started. They're incredibly capable, but they don't know your context, your preferences, or your expectations unless you tell them. The more clearly you communicate, the better the output.

Technique 1 · Give Context and Role

Telling Claude who it is and what context it's in dramatically changes the quality of responses.

❌ Weak Prompt 
Write an email about the meeting.
✅ Strong Prompt 
You are a professional communications assistant. Write a concise, friendly email to my team (5 people) summarizing our Monday meeting. Key decisions: we're moving the product launch to March 15th and Sarah will lead the demo. Tone should be warm but efficient. Max 150 words.

Technique 2 · Chain of Thought

For complex problems, ask Claude to reason step by step before giving the final answer. This reduces errors significantly.

User 
My business makes $12,000/month in revenue. My expenses are: rent $1,800, salaries $6,200, software $450, marketing $800, misc $300. Should I hire a part-time employee at $1,500/month?

Think through this step by step before giving your recommendation.
💡

The phrase "think step by step" or "reason through this before answering" is one of the most powerful tools in prompting. It forces the model to show its work, catch its own errors, and give more reliable answers.

Technique 3 · Few-Shot Examples

Show Claude the exact format or style you want by giving it 1–3 examples. It will match the pattern precisely.

User 
Write product descriptions in this exact style:

Example 1:
Product: Leather Wallet
Description: Slim. Sturdy. Doesn't quit. Holds 8 cards and fits any pocket.

Example 2:
Product: Ceramic Mug
Description: Thick walls. Perfect weight. Your morning ritual just got an upgrade.

Now write one for: Wireless Earbuds

Technique 4 · Specify the Output Format

Format Instructions That Work

  • "Reply in bullet points, max 5 bullets"
  • "Format as a table with columns: Name, Cost, Pros, Cons"
  • "Write this as a numbered list with a header for each item"
  • "Give your answer in JSON format"
  • "Keep the total response under 200 words"
  • "Write at a 7th grade reading level"

Control the Tone & Style

  • "Write in a casual, conversational tone"
  • "Be direct and concise — no filler words"
  • "Use a formal, professional tone"
  • "Write like you're explaining to a 10-year-old"
  • "Be enthusiastic and encouraging"
  • "Mirror the writing style of the text I paste below"

Technique 5 · System Prompts (Advanced)

A system prompt is a hidden instruction that shapes how Claude behaves for the entire conversation. Used by developers building apps, but you can use them in Claude Projects too.

System Prompt (hidden from users) 
You are a helpful assistant for a furniture company called Destec. You specialize in office and ergonomic furniture.

Rules:
- Always recommend consulting a certified ergonomics specialist for medical needs
- Keep responses concise (under 100 words unless asked for more)
- If asked about competitor products, acknowledge them fairly but highlight Destec's strengths
- Always end product recommendations with a call to action to visit the showroom

The Prompt Framework: C.R.E.A.T.E

C — Context

Set the scene. Who are you? What's the situation? What does Claude need to know?

R — Role

Tell Claude what persona to adopt. "You are a senior marketing strategist..."

E — Examples

Show it what good looks like with 1–3 examples of the format or style you want.

A — Ask

State your actual request clearly and specifically. Be explicit about what you want.

T — Type

Specify the output format: bullet list, table, essay, code, JSON, etc.

E — Edit

Iterate! "That's good, but make it more formal" or "Expand point 3 into a full paragraph." AI conversations are a dialogue.

💻 Chapter 6

Local LLMs — AI On Your Own Computer

Run AI models completely offline, for free, with full privacy. It's easier than you think.

🔒

Why Go Local?

  • Privacy — your data never leaves your computer
  • Free — no subscription, no per-token costs
  • Offline — works without internet
  • Uncensored — no content filters (double-edged)
  • Control — customize the model how you want
⚠️

The Trade-offs

  • Less capable — local models lag behind frontier models (Claude, GPT-4)
  • Hardware dependent — needs decent RAM/GPU
  • Slower — especially on CPU-only machines
  • Setup required — not as plug-and-play as Claude.ai
🖥️

Minimum Hardware to Get Started

RAM: 8GB minimum (16GB recommended) · Storage: 5–50GB per model · GPU: Not required but dramatically speeds things up (NVIDIA preferred, Apple Silicon works great)

The Tools You Need

1

Ollama — The Easiest Way to Start

Ollama lets you download and run open-source models with a single terminal command. It's like a package manager for AI models. One command installs a model, one command runs it. Works on Mac, Windows, and Linux.

Terminal — Install and run Llama 3
# Install Ollama from ollama.com, then:
ollama pull llama3.2
ollama run llama3.2

# That's it. You're now chatting with a local AI.
2

LM Studio — GUI for Local Models

If terminals aren't your thing, LM Studio gives you a beautiful desktop app for downloading and running models. It has a chat interface just like Claude.ai, model discovery, and performance monitoring. Download from lmstudio.ai.

3

Open WebUI — Browser-Based Chat Interface

Connect Open WebUI to Ollama to get a web interface for your local models — looks and feels like ChatGPT. Supports multiple models, conversation history, image input, and document upload.

Terminal — Run Open WebUI with Docker
docker run -d -p 3000:8080 \
  --add-host=host.docker.internal:host-gateway \
  ghcr.io/open-webui/open-webui:main

# Then open http://localhost:3000 in your browser
4

AnythingLLM — RAG for Local Files

AnythingLLM lets you chat with your own documents using a local AI model. Drop in PDFs, Word docs, or text files and ask questions about them. Everything stays on your machine.

💡

What is GGUF / Quantization?

Models come in different file formats and sizes. GGUF is the format used by Ollama and LM Studio. Quantization (Q4, Q5, Q8) is a compression technique — Q4 means the model was compressed to use 4-bit numbers, making it smaller and faster but slightly less accurate. For most uses, Q4_K_M or Q5_K_M offers the best balance. Don't overthink it — Ollama handles this automatically.

📖 Chapter 7

Glossary

Every AI term you'll encounter — defined in plain English. Search below.

A
Agent / Agentic AI Concept
An AI that can take sequences of actions on its own — browsing the web, running code, managing files — to complete a multi-step goal without you guiding every step. Claude Code is an example of an agentic AI.
API (Application Programming Interface)
A way for software to talk to other software. The Anthropic API lets developers access Claude's capabilities in their own apps. You pay per token used instead of a monthly fee.
Attention Mechanism
The key innovation inside Transformer models. When processing any word in a sentence, the model "pays attention" to every other relevant word — not just the ones nearby. This is how AI understands context across long documents.
B
Benchmark
A standardized test used to compare AI models. Examples: MMLU (general knowledge), HumanEval (coding), GPQA (graduate-level reasoning). When companies say "state-of-the-art" they usually mean top scores on benchmarks.
C
Context Window
The total amount of text an AI can "see" at once — your conversation history + any documents you've shared. Claude 3.5 Sonnet has 200,000 tokens (~150,000 words). When you exceed it, old parts of the conversation are forgotten.
Constitutional AI (CAI)
Anthropic's technique where the AI evaluates its own responses against a set of principles (the "constitution") during training. This makes Claude safer without relying solely on human raters for every response.
E
Embedding
A way of converting text into a list of numbers (a vector) that captures its meaning. Similar concepts get similar numbers, so AI can find related content even without matching exact words. The foundation of semantic search and RAG.
F
Fine-tuning
Additional training on a pre-trained model using a smaller, specialized dataset to improve performance at a specific task. Like giving an already-smart model a focused internship in one domain (medical, legal, customer service, etc.).
Few-Shot Prompting
Giving the AI 2–5 examples of the input/output format you want within your prompt. The model learns the pattern and applies it to new inputs. No training required — it's all in the prompt.
Frontier Model
The most capable AI models at the leading edge of what's technically possible. Claude Opus, GPT-4o, and Gemini Ultra are frontier models. They're expensive to run and are what smaller/local models aspire to match.
G
GGUF
A file format for running AI models locally (on your own computer). Used by Ollama and LM Studio. Models in GGUF format are usually quantized (compressed) to run on regular hardware.
GPU (Graphics Processing Unit)
A specialized processor originally for gaming graphics, but perfect for AI because it handles thousands of calculations simultaneously. Running AI on a GPU is 10–100x faster than on a CPU.
H
Hallucination
When an AI confidently states something that is factually wrong or entirely made up. The AI has no concept of "I don't know" — it always generates the most plausible-sounding response. This is the most important AI limitation to understand.
RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback)
A training technique where humans rate AI responses, and those ratings are used to train the model to produce better outputs. This is how models learn to be helpful and safe, not just fluent.
I
Inference
The act of running a trained model to generate a response. When you type a message to Claude and it responds, that's inference. Training is the expensive one-time process; inference is the ongoing cost of using the model.
K
Knowledge Cutoff
The date after which the model has no knowledge. Claude's training data has a cutoff date, meaning it doesn't know about events, products, or publications after that date. Always ask the model what its cutoff is for time-sensitive topics.
L
LLM (Large Language Model)
The type of AI model that powers Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, and others. Trained on massive text datasets to predict and generate language. "Large" refers to the number of parameters (billions to trillions).
M
Multimodal
An AI model that can work with multiple types of data — text, images, audio, video. Claude with vision is multimodal because it understands both text and images.
P
Parameters
The numerical values inside a neural network that are adjusted during training. A model with 70 billion parameters has 70 billion adjustable numbers. More parameters generally means more capability, but also more computing power needed to run it.
Prompt
The input you give to an AI model — your question, instruction, or request. Writing effective prompts is called "prompt engineering," though "prompt crafting" is more accurate. It's a skill that dramatically affects output quality.
Q
Quantization
A compression technique that reduces model file size by storing numbers with less precision (e.g., 4-bit instead of 32-bit). Makes models run faster with less memory at the cost of slight quality reduction. Q4_K_M is a popular sweet spot.
R
RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)
A technique that combines a search system with an LLM. When you ask a question, relevant documents are fetched from a database and added to the prompt. The AI uses those documents to answer. This is how "chat with your documents" tools work.
S
System Prompt
Hidden instructions given to the AI at the start of a conversation — before the user says anything. Used to define the AI's persona, rules, and context. When you use a product powered by Claude (like a customer service bot), a system prompt has shaped its behavior.
T
Temperature
A setting (usually 0–1) that controls how "random" or "creative" the AI's output is. Low temperature = consistent and factual. High temperature = more varied and creative. Most chat apps set this automatically. Developers can adjust it via the API.
Token
The basic unit an LLM reads and writes. Roughly ¾ of a word — about 4 characters on average. "Tokenization" is how text gets split into tokens before being processed. Context windows and pricing are measured in tokens.
Transformer
The neural network architecture introduced by Google in 2017 that underlies virtually all modern LLMs. Its key innovation is the "attention mechanism," which lets the model relate any word to any other word in the input, regardless of distance.
V
VRAM (Video RAM)
The memory on a GPU. Running AI models requires fitting the model weights into VRAM. A 7B quantized model needs ~4-6GB VRAM; a 70B needs 40GB+. If a model doesn't fit in VRAM, it offloads to regular RAM — which is much slower.
Z
Zero-Shot Prompting
Asking the AI to do a task without giving any examples — just the instruction. Contrast with few-shot prompting. Works well for simple, well-defined tasks. Claude is particularly strong at zero-shot because of its instruction-following training.
